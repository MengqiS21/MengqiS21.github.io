Conversational AI and Human Perception

I study how people understand and respond to conversational agents, including how anthropomorphic and peer-like designs shape perceptions, confidence, and engagement. My work connects research on human perceptions of AI with the design of agents that support learning.

My current work includes a PRISMA-guided systematic review of anthropomorphism in text- and voice-based conversational agents. I also study older adults' AI literacy needs and how peer-like agents can address affective barriers to learning, drawing on earlier work in computing education.

"Understanding how people perceive AI is part of designing interactions that support their needs and expectations."

Research Questions

  • How is anthropomorphism defined, theorized, designed, and measured in conversational agent research?
  • How do people perceive human-like and peer-like conversational agents?
  • How can conversational agents support older adults' confidence, engagement, and understanding of AI?

Selected Project Areas

  • A PRISMA-guided systematic review of 99 studies on anthropomorphism in text- and voice-based conversational agents.
  • Peer conversational agents for older adults' AI literacy, including interviews, qualitative analysis, and design guidelines.
  • Related work on concept inventories and the assessment of conceptual understanding in computing education.
