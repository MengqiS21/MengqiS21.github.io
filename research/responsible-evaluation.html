AI Safety and Support-Seeking

I study how conversational AI can support emotional needs while preserving user agency. My work examines older adults' emotional support-seeking, their experiences of safety interventions, and the design of transitions from AI conversations to outside help.

Across interview studies and experimental research, I investigate how social context, emotional pacing, and actionable guidance shape supportive interactions. I am interested in both how users experience a handoff and whether it supports their intention to seek help beyond the conversation.

"Supportive AI should respect people's agency within a conversation and help them access support beyond it."

Research Questions

  • When do older adults turn to conversational AI for emotional support, and how do social circumstances shape those decisions?
  • How do safety interventions affect emotional pacing, dignity, and user agency?
  • How can AI handoffs support transitions to outside help, and how do perceived transition quality and help-seeking intention differ?

Selected Project Areas

  • AI-to-human handoff strategies grounded in the Theory of Planned Behavior, examined through a 180-participant between-subjects experiment.
  • Interviews with 18 adults aged 60+ on emotional support-seeking, reliance, self-disclosure, and control in conversational AI.
  • User-centered design of safety boundaries and transitions that respond to social context and emotional needs.
